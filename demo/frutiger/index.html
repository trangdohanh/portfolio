ADR F R U T I G E R AN

1928 - 2015

was an influential Swiss designer who created many popular typefaces in addition to his design works. He is considered the “measurer and standard-setter” in the typographic field (Osterer and Stamm 6). Although he grew up in a period of economic uncertainty, his passion for reading, drawing, and painting led him to pursue more artistic and graphical works against his parents' opposition. Frutiger applied for and received a typesetting apprenticeship at a book and fine art printer, which allowed him to further study drawing and woodcuts. After this period and his short employment at a printing plant, Frutiger was accepted into the Kunstgewerbeschule, a vocational art school, in Zurich. He continued learning type design under different teachers. Their knowledge and principles influenced how he eventually designed typefaces (Osterer and Stamm pp. 12-18).

Frutiger worked with both serifs and sans-serifs, as he considered roman fonts to be better reading aids and grotesque fonts harder to draw (Schwemer-Scheddin). Within the large library of typefaces he created, he might be best known for his sans-serifs Univers, Frutiger, and Avenir. He first earned critical success with the serif Méridien, but
UNIVERS gave him international fame. Univers is the first extensive font family, consisting of twenty-one variations designed and released simultaneously. The variety allows designers to explore more possibilities (Frutiger 14). Thanks to its versatility and elegance, Univers can still be seen in signs, logos, merchandise, and more. Legibility and application were thoroughly considered when Frutiger designed a typeface. His adaptability to emerging materials and technologies was one important factor contributing to his influence. Although he started out working with print, with the development of technology, he also paid attention to how texts function with machines. His typeface OCR-B has become the universal standard for optical character recognition. The alphabet is drawn on a finer grid compared to its predecessor OCR-A (Frutiger 53). OCR-B is recognizable by machines and still retains a pleasing aesthetic to the human eyes, despite not being stylized. Frutiger also reworked many of the typefaces he created so they can be used in different applications. One example is his adaptation of Univers for the typewriter IBM Composer (Osterer and Stamm 190).

Frutiger was interested in symbols and challenging the confine of letterforms. Aside from typefaces, he designed logos, wordmarks, and signs. He commissioned signage that is still in use today. The lettering system for the Charles de Gaulle Airport in Paris is Frutiger's work. He also designed for the Paris Métro, although some signs have been redone by a different designer since. He specifically developed the Alphabet Roissy for the Charles de Gaulle Airport, and the Alphabet Métro for the Paris Métro (Osterer and Stamm pp. 224, 244). Outside of Paris, his fonts can be seen in various applications in many parts of the world.
